## Integrating Federal Decree-Law No. 17 of 2019, Cabinet Decision No. 43 of 2021, and Federal Law No. 10 of 2025

## Executive Summary

The United Arab Emirates has consolidated its strategic-goods, AML/CFT, and proliferation-
financing compliance obligations across three legislative instruments:

| Instrument | Subject matter | Primary authority |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Decree-Law No. 17 of 2019 | Control of military goods, dual-use items, and strategic technology exports | UAE Ministry of Economy / Supreme Committee for Strategic Goods Control |
| Cabinet Decision No. 43 of 2021 | Implementing regulation for AML/CFT/CPF; customer due diligence, beneficial ownership, targeted financial sanctions | CBUAE, UAE FIU (AMLSCU), NAMLCFTC |
| Federal Law No. 10 of 2025 | Updated compliance framework — verify scope at official source | [Authority: verify at moj.gov.ae] |

Entities operating in the UAE — or transacting with UAE counterparties from outside the UAE —
face a three-layer compliance obligation: (1) UAE-law requirements under the above instruments;
(2) US jurisdictional **reach-through** via the Foreign Direct Product Rule (FDPR), OFAC
secondary sanctions, and BIS end-use/entity-level controls; and (3) international standards
(FATF Recommendations, UN Security Council Resolutions on targeted sanctions).

This framework consolidates all three layers into a single requirements matrix, a phased
implementation roadmap, and a board-ready governance structure.

## Part 1 — Regulatory Architecture

### 1.1 Federal Decree-Law No. 17 of 2019 — Military and Strategic Goods Control

The UAE's strategic-goods control regime governs the export, re-export, import, transit, and
brokering of **military goods**, **dual-use items**, and **related technology and software**.
Key structural features include:

- **Positive licensing requirement:** A permit from the competent authority is required before
  exporting, re-exporting, or brokering listed items. The default position is prohibition;
  licensing is the exception.

- **Scope of "strategic goods":** Covers items on the UAE's Military List (broadly equivalent
  to the Wassenaar Arrangement Munitions List) and Dual-Use List (broadly equivalent to
  Wassenaar dual-use annexes), as well as software and technology for the development,
  production, or use of listed items.

- **Re-export control:** A UAE importer who subsequently re-exports strategic goods requires
  a separate re-export permit. This obligation applies regardless of whether the original
  export was permitted by a foreign jurisdiction.

- **Technology and intangible transfer:** The law covers intangible technology transfer —
  electronic, visual, and oral release of controlled technical data to foreign persons — as
  well as physical commodity export.

- **Brokering:** Brokering services (arranging, financing, or facilitating transactions in
  listed items) by UAE persons or from UAE territory are covered, even where the goods never
  physically enter the UAE.

- **End-user undertaking (EUU):** Exports of listed items may require an end-user certificate
  and undertaking from the importing party as a precondition of licence issuance.

!!! info "Interaction with US EAR"
    Items that are simultaneously controlled under the UAE strategic-goods regime and the US
    EAR require a UAE export permit AND a US export licence (or applicable licence exception).
    The two regimes operate independently. A UAE permit does not satisfy a US EAR licence
    requirement, and vice versa. Where an item is subject to the US FDPR, an EAR licence
    requirement may apply even where the item was produced entirely outside the United States.
    See Part 3 (US Reach-Through) below.

### 1.2 Cabinet Decision No. 43 of 2021 — AML/CFT/Proliferation Financing

Cabinet Decision No. 43 of 2021 provides the implementing regulation for the UAE's AML/CFT
framework (which rests principally on the Federal Decree-Law on Anti-Money Laundering and
Combatting the Financing of Terrorism, as amended). Key obligations include:

- **Risk-based approach:** Regulated entities must implement a risk-based AML/CFT/CPF
  programme calibrated to their size, nature, and risk exposure. A documented risk assessment
  is required.

- **Customer due diligence (CDD):** Identity verification, beneficial ownership identification,
  and understanding of the business relationship are mandatory at onboarding and on a risk-
  proportionate basis throughout the relationship.

- **Enhanced due diligence (EDD):** Required for high-risk categories: politically exposed
  persons (PEPs), correspondent banking relationships, non-face-to-face customers, and
  counterparties in high-risk jurisdictions (FATF grey/black list countries; UAE National Risk
  Assessment high-risk sectors).

- **Targeted financial sanctions (TFS):** Immediate asset-freeze and prohibition obligations
  apply on designation by the UN Security Council (resolutions 1267, 1373, and successors),
  the UAE NAMLCFTC local list, and any other list specified by Cabinet resolution. Unlike
  OFAC, UAE TFS obligations are triggered without a separate national implementation step for
  UN designations.

- **Proliferation financing (CPF/PF):** Regulated entities must apply targeted financial
  sanctions specifically for proliferation financing (UN Security Council Resolution 1718 for
  DPRK; UNSCR 2231 for Iran), conduct PF risk assessments, and implement controls
  commensurate with assessed PF risk.

- **Suspicious transaction reporting (STR):** Mandatory reporting to the UAE Financial
  Intelligence Unit (AMLSCU) of any transaction, customer, or pattern that gives rise to
  reasonable grounds for suspicion of money laundering, terrorism financing, or proliferation
  financing. The tipping-off prohibition applies.

- **Recordkeeping:** Transaction records, CDD files, and compliance records must be retained
  for **five years** from the end of the business relationship or transaction; STR reports for
  five years from the date of filing.

## Part 2 — US Jurisdictional Reach-Through

A UAE-licensed entity is not insulated from US export-control and sanctions law. The following
US-origin nexuses can subject a UAE-nexus transaction to concurrent US jurisdiction:

| US mechanism | Trigger | Relevant authority |
|---|---|---|
| **EAR jurisdiction** | Item was produced in the US, incorporates US-origin content above de minimis, or is produced using US technology/software/equipment (FDPR) | 15 CFR §§ 734.3, 734.4, 734.9 |
| **BIS Entity List** | A party to the transaction appears on the Entity List, or is 50%+ owned by a listed party (BIS Affiliates Rule — note suspension until 9 Nov 2026) | 15 CFR §§ 744.11, 744.21 |
| **OFAC primary sanctions** | Any US person, US-dollar transaction, or US-origin item is involved | 31 CFR programme regulations |
| **OFAC secondary sanctions** | Non-US person transacts with SDN-listed party or sanctioned sector (Russia/CAATSA; Iran/IFCA; DPRK/CAATSA) | IEEPA; CAATSA; CISADA |
| **OFAC 50% Rule** | Counterparty is 50%+ owned (aggregated) by SDN-listed parties | OFAC Aug 2014 guidance |
| **Deemed export / deemed re-export** | Foreign person in the UAE accesses US-controlled technology or source code | 15 CFR §§ 734.13–734.16 |

!!! danger "BIS Affiliates Rule snap-back: 10 November 2026"
    The BIS Affiliates Rule (EAR § 744.21) is suspended under Phase 1 until 9 November 2026.
    On 10 November 2026, Phase 2 reimposition is currently scheduled. UAE-based entities that
    are 50%+ affiliated with BIS-listed parties must plan for Phase 2 now. Post-snap-back,
    a licence will be required for all EAR-controlled items — including advanced-computing ICs
    and AI model weights — transferred to any such affiliated entity, regardless of their UAE
    legal status. See the [BIS Affiliates Rule](../docs/regulations/bis-affiliates-rule.md)
    page for current monitoring status.

### AI Classification Uncertainty

The classification of AI model weights as controlled technology remains unsettled following
the rescission of ECCN 4E091 on 13 May 2025. BIS has indicated a replacement rule is
forthcoming. In the interim, UAE-nexus AI transactions involving frontier model weights
should be assessed against:

1. The physical compute (chips) used for training and inference — ECCN 3A090.a, 4A090.a
2. The training source code — potential ECCN 4E002 or EAR99 (fact-specific)
3. Any applicable end-use controls (EAR Part 744) where the UAE counterparty has advanced-
   computing or AI activities relevant to military, intelligence, or WMD end-uses
4. The 10 Nov 2026 Affiliates Rule snap-back for UAE affiliates of Entity List parties

Cross-reference: [Compliance Clinic 01 — UAE Affiliate Transfer & PRC/Iran Deemed Exports](
../docs/scenarios/2026-uae-affiliate-deemed-export.md) for a worked example of these issues.

## Part 4 — 12-Week Phased Implementation Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–4)

**Objective:** Achieve regulatory clarity, identify gaps, and establish governance structure.

| Week | Workstream | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regulatory mapping | Confirm applicable UAE law numbers; identify all regulated activities; obtain legal opinion on Law 10-2025 scope |
| 1–2 | Gap analysis | Compare existing controls against Requirements Matrix (Parts 3A and 3B); score each requirement as Compliant / Partially Compliant / Gap |
| 2 | Governance appointments | Designate ECO and MLRO; confirm board reporting lines; establish escalation protocols |
| 3 | Policy drafting | Draft or update: (i) Export Control Policy; (ii) AML/CFT/CPF Policy; (iii) US Reach-Through Policy (FDPR, OFAC, Affiliates Rule); (iv) Deemed-Export Policy |
| 3–4 | Product/item classification | Classify all products, software, and technology against UAE and US control lists; identify ECCN; flag items needing CCATS |
| 4 | Board presentation | Present gap-analysis results and remediation plan to Board or Risk Committee for approval |

**Phase 1 gates:** Board approval of remediation plan; MLRO and ECO appointments confirmed;
gap analysis complete with priority scoring.

### Phase 2 — Implementation (Weeks 5–8)

**Objective:** Close priority gaps, implement systems and controls, train staff.

| Week | Workstream | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Screening systems | Implement or upgrade sanctions/restricted-party screening tool covering: UN, OFAC SDN, UAE local list, BIS consolidated lists; configure OFAC 50% Rule UBO aggregation logic |
| 5–6 | CDD/EDD procedures | Implement updated CDD/EDD procedures incorporating UBO identification to ultimate level; PEP screening; high-risk-jurisdiction protocols |
| 6 | Export-control procedures | Implement pre-export classification review; end-user undertaking template; re-export controls; intangible-transfer screening |
| 6–7 | US reach-through controls | Implement FDPR analysis template; de minimis calculation procedure; deemed-export access controls for foreign nationals; BIS Affiliates Rule affiliate-status assessment |
| 7 | Transaction monitoring | Configure or calibrate transaction-monitoring system with ML/TF/PF-relevant rules; test rule coverage against typologies |
| 7–8 | Staff training | Deliver role-specific training: (i) export-control procedures for operations/logistics; (ii) AML/CFT/CPF/STR for compliance and front-line; (iii) sanctions awareness for all staff |
| 8 | Records framework | Implement records-management framework: 5-year retention for export-control and AML/CFT records; secure storage; access controls |

**Phase 2 gates:** All Critical requirements (A-01 to A-05, B-01 to B-08, C-01 to C-05, C-10)
operationally implemented; staff training complete; systems tested.

### Phase 3 — Validation (Weeks 9–12)

**Objective:** Validate controls, establish ongoing monitoring, and present to board.

| Week | Workstream | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Internal audit | Conduct walkthroughs and testing of all Critical and High requirements; identify residual gaps |
| 9–10 | BIS Affiliates Rule snap-back readiness | Complete Phase 2 snap-back (10 Nov 2026) readiness assessment: identify all UAE counterparties potentially within scope; assess licence requirements; submit licence applications where needed |
| 10 | Annual reporting | Set up annual reporting obligations: BIS annual encryption self-classification report; AML/CFT annual regulator report; UAE strategic-goods permit renewal calendar |
| 11 | External audit scoping | Scope and engage external auditor for annual external compliance audit (Requirement G-05) |
| 11–12 | KPI dashboard activation | Activate board KPI dashboard (Part 5); populate baseline metrics; present to Risk Committee |
| 12 | Board closeout | Present implementation completion report to Board; obtain Board approval of updated policies (G-01, G-10) |

**Phase 3 gates:** Internal audit complete; residual gaps documented with owners and
remediation dates; BIS snap-back readiness assessment approved; board KPI dashboard live.

## Part 6 — Risk Mitigation: Priority Scenarios

### Scenario A — UAE Affiliate of BIS-Listed Entity

**Risk:** A UAE counterparty is 50%+ owned (aggregated) by a BIS Entity List party. Under
the BIS Affiliates Rule, controlled-item transfers to this entity require a BIS licence.
Phase 2 snap-back on 10 Nov 2026 reactivates this obligation if currently suspended.

**Mitigation:** Map ownership chains for all UAE counterparties in advanced-computing,
AI, semiconductor, or energy-tech sectors. Where aggregate Entity-List-party ownership ≥ 50%
(using the same aggregation arithmetic as OFAC's 50% Rule), classify the counterparty as
a Phase 2 affected party. Obtain BIS licence before 10 Nov 2026 or suspend the relevant
controlled-item transfers pending snap-back assessment. See Compliance Clinic 01 for a
worked example.

### Scenario B — Deemed Export to UAE-Based Foreign National

**Risk:** A UAE-based employee or contractor who is a national of a controlled country
(PRC, Iran, Russia) is given access to US-controlled technology or source code at UAE
premises. This constitutes a deemed re-export to that person's country of nationality
(15 CFR § 734.16(b)).

**Mitigation:** Implement a Technology Control Plan (TCP) or equivalent access-control
procedure for all UAE-based foreign nationals with potential access to EAR-controlled
technology. Assess nationality against applicable licence requirements; obtain a licence
or implement access restrictions where required. OFAC's Iran prohibitions (31 CFR Part 560)
apply additionally to Iranian-national access regardless of EAR status.

### Scenario C — AI Classification Uncertainty Under Evolving BIS Rules

**Risk:** BIS's rescission of ECCN 4E091 on 13 May 2025 leaves the classification of
frontier AI model weights unsettled. A UAE-nexus transfer of model weights may be
uncontrolled under current rules but controlled under the forthcoming replacement rule.

**Mitigation:** Classify all model-weight transfers against: (1) current chip controls
(3A090.a, 4A090.a — the compute used to train/run the model); (2) training source code
(ECCN or EAR99 — fact-specific); (3) applicable Part 744 end-use controls. Monitor the
BIS Federal Register for the replacement rule. Where commercial or reputational stakes are
high, seek a BIS Classification Determination (CCATS). Do not assume EAR99 for frontier
model weights pending a completed classification analysis.

### Scenario D — OFAC 50% Rule Hidden Exposure in UAE JV Structure

**Risk:** A UAE joint-venture partner has a multi-tier ownership structure in which SDN-
listed parties collectively hold ≥ 50% of the JV, though no single party holds a majority.
The JV is not separately listed on the SDN List. Transacting with the JV is nevertheless
prohibited under OFAC's 50% Rule.

**Mitigation:** Obtain and map the UBO chain of all UAE JV counterparties to the ultimate
beneficial ownership level. Apply the OFAC aggregation test across all SDN-listed owners.
Where the aggregate SDN-owned stake reaches or exceeds 50%, treat the entity as blocked.
Document the analysis. Engage OFAC for a specific licence or comfort letter where the
analysis is inconclusive.
