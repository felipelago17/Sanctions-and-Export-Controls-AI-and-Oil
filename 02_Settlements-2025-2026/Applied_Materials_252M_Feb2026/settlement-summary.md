# Applied Materials — $252M BIS Civil Penalty (February 2026)

## Key Facts

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Settlement ID | BIS-2026-001 |
| Company | Applied Materials (subsidiary) |
| Agency | Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) |
| Penalty Amount | $252,000,000 |
| Date | February 2026 |
| Enforcement Type | Civil penalty (Final) |
| ECCN | 3B001 |

## Conduct

Applied Materials' subsidiary made unlicensed shipments of semiconductor manufacturing equipment to China. The equipment was classified under ECCN 3B001 (equipment for manufacturing semiconductors with certain performance levels), which requires a license for export to China under National Security (NS) controls.

The shipments were routed through intermediaries in third countries to obscure the ultimate destination. This is the "transshipment" pattern: goods move from the US to a nominally neutral country, then on to China — but the ultimate destination triggers EAR license requirements, and using intermediaries to circumvent this requirement violates 15 CFR §764.2(h) (acting with knowledge that a violation is about to occur).

## Why This Case Matters

**Largest BIS civil penalty for semiconductor equipment to China as of February 2026.** The penalty amount signals escalating enforcement under the post-2022 advanced computing controls regime.

**ECCN 3B001 is the class that controls the *machines that make chips*, not the chips themselves.** Controlling manufacturing equipment creates a chokepoint upstream of the finished chip — even if China develops domestic chip designs, without ECCN 3B001-class lithography and deposition equipment, they cannot manufacture at the advanced nodes where US-controlled AI chips operate.

**Transshipment enforcement pattern.** This case, combined with Operation Gatekeeper (UAE hub for 3A090 GPUs), establishes that BIS is systematically targeting the transshipment architecture used to circumvent destination-based controls.

## Compliance Lessons

1. **Know your distributors' distributors.** The red flag is a distributor who ships to a country with no obvious market for the goods being shipped.
2. **Due diligence is not a one-time transaction.** ECCN 3B001-class equipment requires ongoing post-shipment monitoring where feasible.
3. **Penalty calculation is based on total transaction value, not profit.** A $252M penalty on equipment sales is not the gross margin — it is the gross transaction value multiplied by a statutory multiplier for willful violations.
4. **Voluntary self-disclosure can substantially reduce penalties.** 15 CFR §764.5 provides a mitigation framework; early disclosure typically reduces penalties by 25–50%.
