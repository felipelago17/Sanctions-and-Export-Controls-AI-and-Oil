# Semiconductor Manufacturing Equipment — Case Summaries

*Enforcement actions involving unauthorised export of wafer fabrication equipment, EDA software, and related technology.*

---

## Background

Semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME) cases involve the unauthorised export or reexport of wafer fabrication equipment (WFE), electronic design automation (EDA) software, and process technology essential for advanced chip manufacturing. Key controlled categories:

| Item type | Representative ECCNs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fabrication equipment | 3B001, 3B002 | Includes lithography, deposition, etch, metrology |
| EDA software | 3D001, 3D003 | Design tools for chips ≥ 3nm process node |
| Semiconductor technology | 3E001, 3E002 | Process know-how, device design technology |
| EUV lithography equipment | 3B001.b.1 (US); separate Dutch control | Only ASML produces; critical chokepoint |

The FDPR (15 CFR § 734.9) is frequently at issue in SME cases: tools manufactured outside the United States using US-origin technology or produced in a plant using US-origin equipment may be subject to EAR jurisdiction. See [De Minimis & FDPR Disputes](../../analysis-frameworks/de-minimis-fdr-disputes.md).

Enforcement patterns:

- **Reexport through allied jurisdiction:** US SME legally exported to a Japanese or European manufacturer, then reexported to China without a US reexport licence
- **Technology transfer through training:** US-based engineers providing process training to Chinese fab personnel — a deemed export / deemed reexport
- **Shell company acquisition:** Chinese entity establishes a US shell to acquire SME or EDA tools, then ships them to China
- **Used equipment diversion:** Used SME sold as scrap or through secondary markets, then reconstituted and shipped to controlled fab
