# Russia Transshipment & Straw Buyers — Case Summaries

*Criminal and administrative actions against US-based procurement intermediaries routing controlled goods to Russia through third-country "straw buyer" companies after the February 2022 controls.*

---

## Background

Since the expansion of the Russia/Belarus controls in EAR Part 746 (February–March 2022), the dominant DOJ-NSD export-control fact pattern has been the **domestic procurement agent**: a US-incorporated trading or freight company, often run by a Russian-speaking principal, that keeps serving its pre-war Russian customers by inserting a nominal buyer in a non-sanctioning jurisdiction. The straw buyer appears on the commercial invoice, the Electronic Export Information (EEI/AES filing) and any end-user statement; the goods are forwarded on arrival.

Recurring jurisdictions: **Türkiye, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Armenia, UAE, Hong Kong, South Korea, China**. Recurring goods: electronic components (ECCN 3A991 and EAR99 items on the Common High Priority Items List), aviation parts (9A991), industrial and mining equipment, firearms components.

Standard charging template:

| Count type | Statute | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Conspiracy | 18 U.S.C. § 371 | To violate ECRA and to defraud the US |
| Unlawful export / attempted export | ECRA, 50 U.S.C. § 4819; 15 CFR § 764.2 | Per shipment |
| Smuggling goods from the US | 18 U.S.C. § 554 | Used where the item is EAR99 but the EEI or licence representation is false |
| False statements | 18 U.S.C. § 1001 | Typically to CBP or BIS on end user / licence status |
| Money-laundering conspiracy | 18 U.S.C. § 1956(h) | Proceeds routed through the intermediary's accounts |

The evidentiary core is almost always **email and messaging traffic** showing the parties discussing the third-country routing — satisfying the § 772.1 "knows" standard directly rather than by inference. See [Knowledge Inference Pathways](../../analysis-frameworks/knowledge-inference-pathways.md) and [High-Probability Standard](../../analysis-frameworks/high-probability-standard.md).

## US v. Pankov — Pacific Route Inc. (W.D. Wash., October 2026)

**Respondent:** Ilya Pankov a/k/a Ilia Pankov, 53, of Seattle; principal and registered agent of **Pacific Route Inc.**, a Washington-incorporated procurement company doing business as **Pacific Terminals**.

**Regulator / prosecutors:** US Attorney's Office, Western District of Washington (Acting US Attorney Charles Neil Floyd) with DOJ National Security Division; investigation by Homeland Security Investigations with US Customs and Border Protection (per press reporting; the DOJ release names NSD's involvement).

**Date:** arrested 27 Oct 2026 on a twelve-count indictment; arraigned the same day in US District Court, Seattle; **not guilty** plea; trial set for **4 January 2027** before US District Judge Tana Lin.

**Statutory basis (as described in the release):** conspiracy; unlawful export of controlled goods to Russia; smuggling goods from the United States; false statements; conspiracy to commit money laundering. Twelve counts in total; the count-by-count statute mapping is in the indictment, not the release.

**Controlled items:** "export-controlled mining and other equipment" (MLex summary of the release). ECCNs and values not stated publicly.

**Destination / end users:** multiple companies in Russia (not named in the release).

**Alleged conduct:**

- In **February–March 2022**, as the Russia controls expanded, Pankov and others allegedly agreed to keep supplying Russian customers by shipping first to companies in **South Korea, Türkiye or Kazakhstan**, which forwarded the goods to Russia.
- The indictment identifies **four shipments in 2023 and 2024** as unlawful exports.
- Pankov and others allegedly filed **false export information** (EEI) and misrepresented whether shipments required a licence.
- When **CBP seized two shipments** for sanctions violations, Pankov allegedly told CBP that the Korean company was the end user while knowing the goods were destined for Russia.
- At least **USD 225,000** in proceeds allegedly laundered through accounts connected to Pacific Route.
- The Acting US Attorney's statement stresses that email exchanges record the participants openly discussing the use of third-country businesses to get round the controls.

**Knowledge standard:** actual knowledge, evidenced by the parties' own communications — no reliance on red-flag inference.

**Resolution / penalty:** none yet; allegations only, presumption of innocence applies.

**Cross-references:** [Inchoate Provisions](../../analysis-frameworks/inchoate-provisions-application.md) (conspiracy counts); [Knowledge Inference Pathways](../../analysis-frameworks/knowledge-inference-pathways.md); [EUNPDC South Caucasus analysis](../../../cases/2026-08-eunpdc-south-caucasus-acw-sanctions-compliance.md) for the Kazakhstan/Caucasus transit context from the EU side.

**Why it is indexed here:** a clean, current example of the straw-buyer template — one US intermediary, three interchangeable third-country buyers, false EEI, a false statement to CBP on seizure, and a modest laundering count attached to make the proceeds reachable. The Korea routing is the less common element; Türkiye and Kazakhstan are the standard ones.

**UAE relevance:** the same mechanics are what BIS and OFAC look for in UAE free-zone trading companies serving Russian customers — the straw buyer is simply domiciled in Dubai or RAK instead of Istanbul or Almaty. The case is a useful training example for freight forwarders and trading-house compliance teams on how EEI misstatements and end-user representations become § 1001 and § 554 counts.

**Sources:** DOJ USAO-WDWA press release, 27 Oct 2026 ("Seattle man arrested on indictment for illegally shipping restricted items to Russia by going through straw buyers in Turkey, Korea, and Kazakhstan"); DOJ NSD Export Control and Sanctions News; MLex, 28 Oct 2026; KOMO News; FD Associates October 2026 update.
