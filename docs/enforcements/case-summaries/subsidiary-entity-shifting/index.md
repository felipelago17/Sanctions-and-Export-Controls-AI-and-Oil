# Subsidiary & Entity Shifting — Case Summaries

*Enforcement actions where respondents used shells, subsidiaries, or nominees to transact with Entity List or SDN-listed parties.*

---

## Background

Entity-shifting cases involve the establishment or exploitation of third-country or domestic legal entities to route transactions that would otherwise require a licence because a direct counterparty is on the Entity List, MEU List, or SDN List. The conduct is charged under:

- **15 CFR § 764.2(h):** Evasion — acting with intent to evade EAR provisions
- **15 CFR § 764.2(b):** Conspiracy — agreement among the shifting parties
- **OFAC: 50% Rule** — where the SDN party holds ≥ 50% (direct or aggregate) in the shell, the shell is itself blocked regardless of its separate legal existence

See [OFAC UBO & 50% Rule](../../../regulations/ofac-ubo-50-percent-rule.md) and [BIS Affiliates Rule](../../../regulations/uae/bis-affiliates-rule.md) for the parallel regulatory frameworks that the entity-shifting conduct seeks to circumvent.

### Common Entity-Shifting Structures

| Structure | Description | Enforcement signature |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean-name front** | Newly incorporated entity with no connection to the restricted party on paper; in practice controlled by restricted party principals | New incorporation date; shared address/directors; payments from restricted party |
| **Dormant subsidiary reactivation** | A dormant subsidiary of an Entity List party is reactivated under a changed name to conduct controlled transactions | Corporate registry shows dormancy; reactivation timed with Entity List addition |
| **Layered affiliate chain** | Multiple intermediate entities in different jurisdictions dilute the apparent ownership below the 50% threshold at each link | Aggregate ownership > 50% when traced through chain; OFAC 50% Rule applies |
| **Contractual nominee** | A legitimate third party contractually acts as the named party on export documentation while the restricted party bears economic risk | Contract terms; risk allocation; back-to-back payments |

### BIS Affiliates Rule Interaction

The BIS Affiliates Rule (15 CFR § 744.21) closes the entity-shifting gap for Entity List and MEU List cases: entities owned ≥ 50% individually or in the aggregate by listed parties are themselves subject to licence requirements. The snap-back to full Phase 2 coverage on 10 November 2026 extends this to EAR99 items.

See [BIS Affiliates Rule](../../../regulations/uae/bis-affiliates-rule.md) for the current suspension status and Phase 2 timeline.

## Extreme Networks, Inc. — DOJ-NSD NPA + OFAC settlement (27 October 2026)

**Respondent:** Extreme Networks, Inc. (North Carolina; cloud networking)
**Regulator:** DOJ National Security Division with USAO E.D.N.C.; OFAC (parallel civil settlement); FBI, HSI and BIS as investigative partners
**Date:** 27 October 2026
**Statutory basis:** IEEPA / Russia-related sanctions; ECRA / EAR (unauthorized exports, false export filings); smuggling
**Underlying restricted party:** JSC Moscow Machinery Building Plant Avangard (MMZA), Russian air-defence (S-400) manufacturer — **not itself listed**; blocked by operation of the OFAC 50% Rule as a majority-owned subsidiary of SDN JSC Concern Almaz-Antey
**Shell / nominee entity:** Respondent's own (since-closed) Russian subsidiary and a third-party distributor; a front customer was used to mask MMZA as ultimate end user (press reporting in 2022 named DEMZ). Russia-based employees concealed the end user from global trade compliance.
**50% Rule / Affiliates Rule applicability:** 50% Rule — yes (ownership-derived blocking, no list entry). Affiliates Rule — not charged; conduct pre-dates the rule.
**Controlled items / transactions:** Networking equipment and related services; 11 purchase orders (DOJ) / 9 exports (OFAC), March/September 2018 – March 2022; revenue under $500,000
**Alleged conduct:** Subsidiary personnel routed sales to a 50%-blocked defence end user, generating unauthorized transactions, false export filings and smuggling from the United States
**Knowledge standard applied:** Willful concealment by subsidiary employees; corporate liability resolved on cooperation/remediation record
**Resolution:** Non-prosecution agreement — the **first "near-miss" (CEP Part II) resolution** by NSD under the March 2026 department-wide Corporate Enforcement and Voluntary Self-Disclosure Policy. A declination was unavailable because the disclosure came days before an investigative journalist published (not "timely"); contrast the Bosch declination (June 2026), where disclosure during an ongoing internal investigation qualified.
**Penalty:** DOJ $487,860.92 criminal fine + $487,860.92 forfeiture ($975,721.84); OFAC settlement $1,529,685. Three-year term (longer than the CEP's "fewer than three years" guidance for near-miss cases); no monitor; no protection for individuals. Remediation credited: Russian subsidiary closed, distributor terminated, end-user diligence and denied-party screening enhanced.
**Cross-references:** [OFAC 50% Rule](../../../regulations/ofac-ubo-50-percent-rule.md) · [BIS Affiliates Rule](../../../regulations/uae/bis-affiliates-rule.md) · [M&A diligence manual](../../ma-export-controls-diligence.md) (the subsidiary came with the 2017 Avaya networking acquisition) · [Knowledge inference pathways](../../analysis-frameworks/knowledge-inference-pathways.md)
**Primary sources:** [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/national-security-division-announces-first-near-miss-non-prosecution-agreement-under) · [NPA](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1468856/dl?inline=) · [OFAC enforcement release](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USTREAS/bulletins/430aa89) · [Revised CEP (March 2026)](https://www.justice.gov/dag/media/1430731/dl?inline=)
**Secondary:** Morrison Foerster client alert (2 Nov 2026); Bracewell (30 Oct 2026)

**Why it matters for UAE/GCC groups.** The liability attached to an entity on no list, through ownership alone, and the conduct sat inside an acquired foreign subsidiary that fed false end-user data upward. Both are the exact exposures flagged in the [UAE affiliate clinic](../../../scenarios/2026-uae-affiliate-deemed-export.md): independent UBO verification of counterparties, and post-acquisition integration of trade-compliance controls, not reliance on the subsidiary's own attestations.
