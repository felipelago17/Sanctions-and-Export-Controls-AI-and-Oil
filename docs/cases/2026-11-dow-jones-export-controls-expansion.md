# Analysis — U.S. Export Controls May Expand Through New Laws and Enforcement (Dow Jones Risk & Compliance, Nov 2026)

Ivan Eckhardt, *U.S. Export Controls May Expand Through New Laws, Enforcement* (Dow Jones Risk & Compliance blog, November 2026; the text supplied is undated, and its enforcement data run to 3 November 2026). The piece argues that the US is heading for a broad increase in export-control activity: more legislation, an Affiliates Rule that may bite on a very large population of entities, and a visibly strengthened enforcement apparatus.

> **Source status:** Commercial risk-intelligence commentary, read from a text copy supplied by the repository owner (the web page itself could not be fetched). Dataset figures are Dow Jones's own and were not independently verified; settlement figures are attributed by the article to Gibson Dunn. It **predates the BIS remote-access interim final rule of 16 November 2026** and treats remote-access controls as still pending. Not legal advice.

---

## Key Points

- **Three House Foreign Affairs Committee bills** (MATCH Act, AI OVERWATCH Act, Chip Security Act) are described as likely NDAA riders. The article reads their heavy emphasis on allied alignment as a move toward a harmonised, multilateral Western control framework. Matthew Axelrod (Gibson Dunn; former BIS Assistant Secretary for Export Enforcement) is quoted on the point that controls work best when multilateral and that allied consensus protects US industry from competitive disadvantage.
- **Remote Access Security Act** passed the House in January 2026; if enacted it would extend BIS controls to foreign persons accessing US-jurisdiction items over the internet or cloud.
- **Four further bills** are listed as pending: Export Control Reform Act; Stop Stealing our Chips Act (whistleblower incentives, modelled on DOJ's use of whistleblowers in tariff enforcement); Interagency Coordination in Export Controls Act; Export Control Enforcement and Enhancement Act.
- **Midterms:** the article judges that Democratic control of Congress is unlikely to change the bills' prospects because support is bipartisan.
- **Affiliates Rule:** postponed to **10 January 2027** as part of the US–China trade truce (the "Busan agreement"). Axelrod considers further postponement entirely possible because the rule is tied up with China's rare-earth exports; some companies are nonetheless screening as if the rule were in force, preferring screening cost to the risk of a customer routing goods to an affiliate.
- **Scale:** Dow Jones data show 70+ entries on the MEU list and 3,000+ on the Entity List (mostly Russian and Chinese, with notable numbers from Pakistan, the UAE, Hong Kong and Iran). Its internal affiliate dataset is described by its ownership-research director as "tens of thousands" of entries, heavily APAC. Roughly **30 %** of entities added to the MEU and Entity Lists since 2020 were already owned by BIS-listed companies.
- **Aggregate ownership:** the 50 % test adds up listed owners' stakes (e.g. 35 % + 15 % = 50 %), with the more restrictive standard applying where owners face different restrictions. The article also states the rule reaches certain SDN-owned entities but not the Unverified List or Denial Orders.
- **Enforcement:** nine BIS civil settlements in 2026 totalling **$299.6 million** (as of 3 Nov 2026, per Gibson Dunn data). BIS's FY2027 request would more than double enforcement to **$301 million, 669 staff and 65 overseas officers**.

---

## Why It Matters

1. **Affiliates-rule exposure is a data problem.** The article's figures (tens of thousands of affiliates; 30 % of recent listings already BIS-owned) support screening on ultimate-owner linkages now, rather than on the day the suspension lifts. See [BIS Affiliates Rule](../regulations/uae/bis-affiliates-rule.md) and the [50 % rule analysis](../regulations/ofac-ubo-50-percent-rule.md).
2. **Enforcement capacity is rising.** Settlement volume and the proposed overseas-officer increase point to more investigations of third-country diversion, consistent with the [CSIS enforcement-turn analysis](2026-11-csis-mind-the-gap-bis-enforcement.md).
3. **Legislative and regulatory tracks are converging.** The bills named here are tracked on the [AI-chip legislation tracker](../regulations/us-ai-chip-export-legislation-tracker.md); the remote-access gap the article flags as pending has since been addressed administratively by the [BIS remote-access IFR](2026-11-bis-remote-access-ifr-744-24.md).

## Relevance to UAE and Gulf Compliance Functions

- The article lists the UAE among the significant jurisdictions for Entity List entries, so UAE counterparties are a recognised screening focus.
- Affiliate-level screening (aggregate ownership, not only direct listing) should be part of onboarding for UAE-based distributors, logistics providers and compute customers, whatever happens on 10 January 2027.
- Multilateral alignment language in the bills suggests allied and partner jurisdictions, including Gulf hubs, will face continued pressure to mirror US controls.

## Contested and Unverified Items

- Dow Jones's "tens of thousands" affiliate figure and the 30 % statistic come from proprietary data not disclosed in the article.
- Settlement totals and budget-request figures are as reported; confirm against BIS and Gibson Dunn primary materials before citing.
- Whether the Affiliates Rule takes effect on 10 January 2027 is open; the source itself treats further delay as plausible.
- Bill numbers and statuses should be taken from the tracker, not this article.
