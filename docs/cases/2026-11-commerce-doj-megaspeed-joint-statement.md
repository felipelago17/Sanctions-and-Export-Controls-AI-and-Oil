# Commerce–DOJ Joint Statement on the Megaspeed Investigation (16–17 November 2026)

On **16 November 2026** (posted by Commerce that day; carried by DOJ on 17 November as Press Release 26-1365) the **Department of Commerce** and the **Department of Justice** issued a rare joint statement on their investigation of **Megaspeed**, the Singapore-based data-centre and cloud company suspected of diverting advanced Nvidia AI hardware to China. The statement, issued "to address inaccurate information circulating in public reporting", sets out an official timeline for the first time and confirms that **DOJ now leads a criminal investigation** with BIS.

> **Source and verification status:** The statement is a **primary source** (Commerce and DOJ Office of Public Affairs). The site pages and PDF could not be fetched directly from this workspace; the passages below were taken from search-engine excerpts of the official pages and cross-checked against CNN, NPR, the Washington Times and others. **The full text has not been read end to end.** Anything marked *(press)* rests on news reporting, not on the statement. Allegations against Megaspeed or any individual are **unproven**; no charges have been announced and Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. Not legal advice.

## What the statement says (official timeline)

| Date | Event (as stated by Commerce and DOJ) |
|---|---|
| 2024 | Commerce opens an investigation into Megaspeed for potential export-control violations, after **proactive monitoring** of sensitive-semiconductor diversion flags suspicious activity |
| May 2025 | Commerce investigators request records from Nvidia on **direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed** |
| June 2025 | Nvidia places Megaspeed on its internal **"no sell" list** |
| June 2025 | BIS agents, **with Singaporean law enforcement**, conduct an **on-site, in-person end-use check** at Megaspeed and interview its then-CEO, **Alice Huang** |
| After the check | "Following this enforcement action", **DOJ takes the lead** in the criminal investigation |
| Going forward | DOJ and BIS "working together" will pursue the full extent of the law for any criminal conduct that may have occurred |

The statement also asserts a track record: under the current administration, **17 defendants across six separate investigations** have been charged in connection with semiconductor-smuggling allegations (opening sentence retrieved; remainder of the paragraph not read).

## What the statement is responding to

The statement follows a week of reporting *(press)*:

- **NYT, 9 Nov 2026:** Commerce's probe halted in 2025 and moved to DOJ after Nvidia's general counsel emailed BIS agents citing an under secretary's permission to contact them; some officials feared the contact could taint the case.
- **FT, 10 Nov 2026:** four other US agencies pushed for Megaspeed to be added to the **Entity List**; Commerce has not done so (and reportedly has added no company in over a year).
- **Megaspeed, 12–13 Nov 2026:** says it has hired a US law firm for a "comprehensive census" of its GPU assets and has cooperated with BIS inspections.

In the excerpts retrieved, the statement fixes **when** things happened (2024 start; May/June 2025 steps) but I did not see it address the Nvidia general counsel's contact, the Entity List question, or Megaspeed's ownership. Whether it does so in the unread portions is **unverified**. Commentary noted that it does not mention the Nvidia CEO *(press)*.

## Why it matters for export-control and sanctions practice

1. **Criminal, not administrative.** The matter has moved from BIS administrative enforcement (Part 766 / civil penalties) to a DOJ criminal track. Corporate counterparties and suppliers should treat contact from either agency as a potential criminal-exposure event.
2. **Vendor-side controls are visible to the government.** The statement publicly records the sequence *records request → vendor "no sell" listing → end-use check*. An OEM or distributor's internal "no sell" or hold list is now part of the evidentiary story; its timing relative to a government request will be read closely.
3. **End-use checks with foreign law enforcement.** A joint BIS–Singapore on-site check, with an interview of the CEO, shows the intended model for transshipment hubs: host-state cooperation rather than unilateral action. Gulf hubs serving advanced-compute tenants should assume the same model can reach them.
4. **No listing needed for liability.** Megaspeed is **not** on the Entity List *(press)*; the investigation proceeds on conduct. Screening that relies on list hits alone would not have flagged it. See the [screening methodology](../screening/index.md) and [OFAC/BIS 50 % analysis](../regulations/ofac-ubo-50-percent-rule.md).
5. **Remote access and cloud.** The Megaspeed fact pattern (cloud capacity serving Chinese users) is the one addressed by the [BIS remote-access IFR](2026-11-bis-remote-access-ifr-744-24.md) and the pending Remote Access Security Act; see also [H200 licensing and vetting](2026-11-nyt-h200-china-licensees-vetting.md).

### Relevance to UAE and Gulf compliance functions

- Providers of GPU capacity to non-resident customers should evidence **customer ownership, location and end-use** at onboarding and refresh, and be able to show when they acted on any government or supplier inquiry.
- Expect **supplier-initiated terminations** (no-sell lists) to precede regulatory action; contracts should anticipate sudden supply cut-off and information requests.
- Inquiries from a host-state authority alongside US agents is a realistic scenario for any regional hub.
