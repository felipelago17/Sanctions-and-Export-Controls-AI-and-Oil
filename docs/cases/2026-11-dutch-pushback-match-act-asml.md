# Dutch Government Rejects the MATCH Act's Extraterritorial Reach over ASML (18 November 2026)

On **18 November 2026** Politico Europe reported that the **Dutch Foreign Ministry** had rejected the US **MATCH Act** (H.R. 8170 / S. 4281), the bipartisan bill that would push allies to align their controls on advanced chipmaking equipment sold and serviced in China. The ministry's stated concern is the bill's **extraterritorial reach** over Dutch companies, above all **ASML**. The Dutch had already opposed the bill in May 2026; this is a renewed and sharper public position.

## Why it matters for export-control practice

1. **Alignment by statute, not by agreement.** The Dutch controls on lithography and servicing rest on national licensing (and the Wassenaar/EU framework). A US statute that would trigger unilateral US extension of controls to Dutch-supplied equipment is the clearest current example of the **extraterritoriality conflict** that already shapes the [Affiliates Rule](../regulations/uae/bis-affiliates-rule.md) and foreign-direct-product debates.
2. **Servicing is the live pressure point.** The reported focus on servicing machines already installed in China means obligations would reach **after-sales support and spare parts**, not only new shipments. Suppliers and service contractors should map installed-base contracts.
3. **Dual-compliance exposure.** A non-US supplier could face a US extension of controls and a home-state position that rejects it. Contract clauses (change-in-law, export-licence conditions, suspension rights) deserve review. Whether any EU or Dutch blocking or countermeasure instrument could be invoked is **not addressed in the sources read**.
4. **Read together with the remote-access rule.** The BIS [remote-access interim final rule](2026-11-bis-remote-access-ifr-744-24.md) reaches compute; MATCH reaches the equipment that makes chips. Both extend US reach beyond US territory, and both draw allied and hub-country reactions.

### Relevance to UAE and Gulf compliance functions

- Gulf fabs, data-centre builders and OEMs buying Dutch- or Japanese-origin tools should expect **supplier-side contract changes** if allied alignment hardens.
- The episode shows how **allies' positions** can differ from US statute; counterparties' home-state rules should be mapped alongside US requirements.
