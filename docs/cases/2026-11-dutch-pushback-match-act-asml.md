# Dutch Government Rejects the MATCH Act's Extraterritorial Reach over ASML (17 November 2026)

On **17 November 2026** (7:18 pm CET) Politico Europe reported that the **Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs** had pushed back on the US **MATCH Act** (H.R. 8170 / S. 4281), the bipartisan bill that would press allies to align controls on the sale and servicing of advanced chipmaking equipment in China. A ministry spokesperson, speaking to Politico on Tuesday 17 November, named the bill's **extraterritorial character** as the main concern and called such measures unacceptable between partners. The stakes are highest for **ASML**.

> **Source and verification status:** The Politico Europe article (Martuscelli, Gijs and Haeck) was supplied by the user as pasted text and read in full; it is **secondary press reporting**, paraphrased here with only short quotes. The Dutch ministry's own statement and the bill text were **not** read. Several other outlets republished the story on 18 November, which is why some reports carry that date. Allegations about individuals mentioned in the article are **unproven**. Not legal advice.

## Why it matters for export-control practice

1. **Alignment by statute, not by agreement.** Dutch controls rest on national licensing. A US statute that would trigger unilateral US extension of controls to Dutch-supplied equipment is the clearest current example of the **extraterritoriality conflict** shaping the [Affiliates Rule](../regulations/uae/bis-affiliates-rule.md) and foreign-direct-product debates.
2. **Servicing is the live pressure point.** The focus on machines already installed in China means obligations would reach **after-sales support and spare parts**, not only new shipments. Suppliers and service contractors should map installed-base contracts.
3. **Dense supply chains.** The article stresses that ASML's tools have tens of thousands of parts from suppliers worldwide, so effects would spread beyond ASML.
4. **Dual-compliance exposure.** A non-US supplier could face a US extension of controls and a home-state position that rejects it. Contract clauses (change-in-law, licence conditions, suspension rights) deserve review. Whether any EU or Dutch countermeasure could be invoked is **not addressed in the article**.
5. **Read with the remote-access rule.** The BIS [remote-access interim final rule](2026-11-bis-remote-access-ifr-744-24.md) reaches compute; MATCH reaches the equipment that makes chips. Both extend US reach beyond US territory.

### Relevance to UAE and Gulf compliance functions

- Gulf fabs, data-centre builders and OEMs buying Dutch- or Japanese-origin tools should expect **supplier-side contract changes** if allied alignment hardens.
- The dispute shows allies' home-state positions can differ from US statute; map counterparties' home-state rules alongside US requirements.
