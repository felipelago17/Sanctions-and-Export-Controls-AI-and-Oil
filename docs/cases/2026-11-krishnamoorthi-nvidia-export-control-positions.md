# Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi on Nvidia and AI-Chip Export Controls (Positions to 24 November 2026)

Rep. **Raja Krishnamoorthi** (D-Illinois, 8th District; a long-time member of the House Select Committee on China and its ranking member through 2025) has been one of the most consistent congressional voices on AI-chip export controls, and was prominent in the week of 16–23 November 2026 after the Nvidia and Megaspeed revelations, the BIS remote-access rule and Nvidia's decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO. This note collects his stated positions as a reference for how congressional oversight is likely to shape chip-export enforcement. It is a record of **his stated views**, not an endorsement and not a finding on any company.

> **Source and verification status:** Sources are search results and fetch summaries (committee press pages, Bloomberg, RTE, The Hill, Daily Kos). **The Hill op-ed of 23 Nov could not be fetched; only its search excerpt was read.** The Daily Kos piece is a community diary relying partly on the author's recollection of dates, so its items are treated as indicative. **No single "speech" of the week could be identified**; the week's material is op-ed, social-media posts and interviews. His current committee role was not confirmed in the sources read. Not legal advice.

## Earlier positions (2025 to autumn 2026; committee releases and press)

- **Revenue-sharing licences (Jul–Nov 2025):** with Rep. Meeks, wrote to Commerce that export controls should not be bargaining chips in trade talks; called the 15 % revenue-share for H20 and MI308 licences a "dangerous misuse" of export controls and wrote to the President that it conflicts with the Export Control Reform Act's ban on licence fees and with the Constitution; introduced with Rep. Kamlager-Dove a bill against licence "pay-to-play".
- **Statutory limits on chip sales:** the No Advanced Chips for the CCP Act (with Rep. Bera, Aug 2025), requiring congressional approval before advanced AI chips go to China, with defined performance thresholds; cosponsor of the RESTRICT Act led by Rep. Meeks (Dec 2025).
- **Enforcement and smuggling:** co-lead with Reps. Foster, Huizenga and Moolenaar (R) of the Chip Security Act (location verification, mandatory diversion reporting; May 2025); cosponsor with Moolenaar of the Stop Stealing Our Chips Act (whistleblower protection; Dec 2025).
- **H200 to China (Dec 2025):** called the plan to allow H200 sales a profound national-security mistake and urged stronger guardrails instead.
- **Gulf (20 Nov 2025):** said authorising Blackwell-class chip exports to Saudi and UAE firms "with deep ties to Beijing" was an extraordinary national-security mistake without guardrails or meaningful concessions. **Relevant to UAE compliance functions**: he treats ties to Beijing, not geography, as the risk.
- **Remote access (autumn 2026 hearing):** pressed experts on reports that Chinese AI firms reached restricted chips remotely through South-East Asian data centres and on a Chinese model reportedly trained on Blackwell chips.
- **Multilateral/AI Diffusion (Jan 2025):** with Chairman Moolenaar supported the AI Diffusion framework while asking for red lines against doing business with CCP-affiliated entities.

## Reading the positions for compliance practice

1. **Direction of travel:** his stated aim is to move chip-export discipline from agency discretion into statute (approval requirements, location verification, remote-access authority); the November 2026 NDAA end-game is the vehicle he names.
2. **Likely oversight questions:** how restricted chips reach China via integrators and shell customers; whether licensing decisions followed political donations; whether companies lobbied against controls. These match fact patterns in the [FT investigation note](2026-11-ft-alice-huang-chip-smuggling-network.md) and the [Megaspeed joint statement](2026-11-commerce-doj-megaspeed-joint-statement.md).
3. **Gulf exposure:** his November 2025 statement shows scrutiny of Gulf AI-chip authorisations; Gulf operators should expect questions about Chinese ties, ownership and customers.
4. **Limits:** statements are advocacy by one member of the minority party; the bills named were not enacted as of the sources read, and the NDAA text is unsettled.
