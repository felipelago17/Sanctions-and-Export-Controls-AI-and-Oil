# UK Notice to Exporters 2026/25: Cryptographic OIELs to Be Discontinued from 1 December 2026

The UK **Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU)** published Notice to Exporters 2026/25 on **19 November 2026**, announcing that it will stop using **cryptographic Open Individual Export Licences (OIELs)** from **1 December 2026**. Exporters of goods or technology with cryptographic functions will need a **military OIEL** or a **dual-use OIEL**, as appropriate, and must describe products by **category with an explanation**, not by individual line item. This is the UK counterpart to the US encryption and advanced-technology controls tracked elsewhere in this repository.

> **Source and verification status:** The notice was fetched from GOV.UK; the tool returned a structured summary of the page, not a line-by-line copy, so section wording and the example categories should be checked against the original. A LinkedIn post by a fractional general counsel (Peter Ho, c. 24 Nov 2026, supplied as a screenshot) was also read; its post-quantum-cryptography (PQC) points are the author's opinion, not the notice. ## What the notice says

| Item | Content (per the fetched summary) |
|---|---|
| Publisher and date | Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade with the ECJU; 19 Nov 2026 |
| Change | ECJU is ending cryptographic OIELs after a review of some licence types; effective **1 Dec 2026** |
| Who is affected | Exporters of goods or technology incorporating cryptographic functions; applicants with live cryptographic OIEL applications, who have been told about transitional arrangements directly |
| Replacement | Apply for a **military OIEL** or a **dual-use OIEL**, as appropriate |
| Application content | ECJU needs more information for its advisers to assess applications; do **not** list individual line items, give a **category of items with an explanation** |
| Worked example | Information-security equipment designed or modified to use digital cryptography (excluding cryptanalysis), with categories such as access points and bridges, adaptive security appliances (limited to devices that control network traffic, stop attacks and enable secure remote access), appliance sensors, application and content networking systems, network modules and routers |
| Transitional process | Only that live applicants have been notified directly; no further detail |
| Existing licences | The notice does **not** say whether existing cryptographic OIELs remain valid after 1 Dec 2026, or for how long |
| OGELs | The notice does **not** mention Open General Export Licences or OGEL alternatives |
| Contact | ECJU, Old Admiralty Building, London SW1A 2DY; exportcontrol.help@businessandtrade.gov.uk; 020 7215 4594; GOV.UK email alerts for notices |

## Practitioner commentary (LinkedIn, Peter Ho, c. 24 Nov 2026; opinion)

- Frames the change as covering cryptographic goods, software or technology leaving the UK, **including know-how shared with overseas teams**.
- Argues it matters beyond export teams because organisations moving to post-quantum cryptography are effectively rebuilding their cryptographic product line: PQC-enabled routers, VPNs and hardware security modules shipped from the UK, and the firmware, keys, source code and engineering expertise UK teams send abroad, would be controlled; narrowly drafted licence categories could block a later quantum-safe release.
- Suggests mapping cryptographic assets and UK outbound flows of hardware and know-how with their licensing status, drafting OIEL categories that anticipate hybrid and PQC roadmaps, and **checking OGEL eligibility** before defaulting to bespoke licences.

These suggestions are the post's author's, not the ECJU's; the notice itself is silent on PQC and on OGELs.

## Why it matters

1. **Licence-type change, not a control-list change.** The notice concerns which licence route is used; it does not, in the summary read, change what is controlled.
2. **Descriptions drive scope.** Category-with-explanation applications put the weight on how products are described; narrow wording can later exclude new product lines.
3. **Gaps for planning.** Silence on existing-licence validity and on OGELs leaves a compliance gap around 1 December that exporters should resolve with ECJU.

### Relevance to UAE and Gulf compliance functions

- UK-origin or UK-developed encryption products, firmware or engineering support supplied to Gulf entities (including group companies and technology partners) now depend on the licence route the supplier holds; ask suppliers which licence covers shipments to the UAE after 1 December.
- Treat UK technology transfers to overseas teams (remote support, source code access) as potential controlled exports when contracting with UK vendors.
- Interaction with US encryption rules (EAR Category 5 Part 2) and with the UAE's own regime is separate and not addressed in the notice.
